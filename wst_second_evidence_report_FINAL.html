⚠ Critical Finding

This report was built entirely from NAWASA's own public Facebook posts — official advisories, valve regulation notices, and system alerts — cross-referenced with timestamped community observations. No data in this report was fabricated or estimated. Every finding is traceable to a public institutional source. The community would not know this pattern exists without someone connecting the dots across 134 days. Water Signal Tracker™ connects those dots.

This Second Evidence Report documents 35+ NAWASA advisories issued between February 13 and June 17, 2026 — now 134 consecutive days — affecting communities across Grenada. Unlike the First Evidence Report, which documented community-submitted signal data, this report is built entirely from institutional source material: official NAWASA notices, valve regulation schedules, broken main alerts, and system recharge announcements. The data reveals a systemic pattern of cascading service restoration variances, missed restoration timelines, inadequate community notification, and a disconnection between declared service availability and actual household water access. One community — Morne Delice, St. George's — appears in NAWASA's own notices as early as March 2, 2026, and remains unresolved as of the date of this report.

Institutional Pattern Findings
F1
The Crisis Began in February — Not May
The first documented advisory affecting the Morne Delice area dates to February 13, 2026 — St. Paul's and surrounding communities. By March 2, NAWASA issued a formal valve regulation schedule specifically naming La Borie, Mt. Airy, Morne Delice, Mt. Parnassus, The Bocas, Creighton, Holder Hill, and Parade under the Les Avocats Water System. The crisis that community reports captured in May was not new — it had been building institutionally for at least 95 days before the First Evidence Report was published.
F2
NAWASA Issued Disconnection Notices During Active Supply Disruption
On May 26, 2026, while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation and multiple communities had been without water for weeks, NAWASA issued a notice advising that disconnection officers would be operating in listed communities — threatening service disconnection for accounts over 30 days in arrears. Customers were being notified of potential disconnection for a service they were not receiving. No exemption or acknowledgment of the ongoing supply disruption was included.
F3
Production Conditions Were Known Since May 13 — Disclosed 23 Days After Communities Were Already Affected
NAWASA's comprehensive dry season advisory on May 13, 2026 disclosed production deficits of 40% at Les Avocats, 47% at Petit Etang, and 69% at Bon Accord — facilities that had been operating below capacity since at least early May. Community members in areas served by these systems had already been without consistent water for weeks before this disclosure.
F4
Advisory Restoration Timelines Were Routinely Revised Without Follow-Up
Across the 134-day record, multiple advisories included specific restoration estimates that required revision: May 27 advisory estimated restoration by 2:00 PM May 28 for the Corinth/La Sagesse corridor; May 29 advisory estimated restoration by 4:00 PM May 30 for Cuma/Hope/Mt. Fann; June 4 advisory estimated restoration by 11:00 AM June 5 for Morne Delice system — confirmed unrestored as of June 6 at 12:17 AM. In most instances, no follow-up advisory was issued when an original timeline required revision.
F5
Communities Were Omitted From Advisories Despite Active Disruption
Multiple instances documented where communities experienced disruption without appearing in official notices: Morne Delice on April 4 — resident confirmed Day 6 without water, community not on NAWASA's published list that day; Grenville on June 5 — public comment confirmed water gone since before midday, Grenville not included in the June 4 advisory; Hope Vale — community member commented on March 19 Morne Delice valve regulation post: "But Hope Vale hasn't had water at..." — confirming disruption outside the official schedule window. The advisories systematically undercount the affected population.
F6
The Brandon Hall Dam Tampering Incident — A National Water Security Event With No Definitive Restoration Timeline
On May 14, 2026, the Brandon Hall Dam was deliberately tampered with, resulting in complete water loss from the facility. NAWASA stated: "the Authority is unable to provide a definitive timeframe for restoration." This event affected La Digue, Holy Innocence, Union, Battle Hill, Caruth, The Villa, Harford Village, and Cook Hill, and placed additional cascading pressure on an already stressed network.
F7
St. George's University Deployed Its Own Water Tanker — Confirming Supplementary Response Was Required
In the May 13 comprehensive advisory, NAWASA disclosed that St. George's University had officially deployed its water tanker and operator to assist with water deliveries in the southern network — indicating that supplementary response capacity was required to support service delivery during this period.
F8
Advisories Were Issued in Silos — No Single Notice Connected the Full Island Picture
Every advisory in this 134-day record addresses a single system, a single community cluster, or a single incident. Not one advisory connects the cumulative impact across all 26 systems. A resident reading any individual notice would have no way of knowing that 12 systems were simultaneously under regulation or that the same communities were being affected by overlapping service variances. Water Signal Tracker™ is the only platform that connects this data across systems, parishes, and time.
F9
"Effective Immediately and Until Further Notice" — Open-Ended Regulation Language
Multiple valve regulation schedules in this record were issued with the phrase "effective immediately and until further notice" — including the March 19 Morne Delice Valve Regulation Schedule and the May 26 Munich Water System notice. This language provides no restoration commitment, no review date, and no planning horizon for affected communities.
F10
⚠ New Finding · June 2026
Restoration Timeline Revision Events and Community Service Outcomes
On June 15, 2026, NAWASA issued an advisory for the Petite Bacaye / Red Gate / La Calome / Old Westerhall / Westerhall Heights corridor in St. David, with a restoration estimate of 8:00 PM that evening. On June 16, a follow-up notice revised the restoration timeline to 12:00 noon. Community-reported service conditions indicate water was restored to the corridor approximately 27 hours after the original estimate. Initial restoration was followed by community reports of reduced pressure.

This sequence documents a distinction between announced restoration timelines and observed household service conditions — the core measurement gap Water Signal Tracker™ was designed to make visible.

Notably, this event marks the first instance in the 134-day record where NAWASA issued a follow-up communication when an original restoration estimate required revision. This represents an observable change in communication behavior worth tracking going forward.
⚠ Accountability Finding — Disconnection Notice During Active Supply Disruption
NAWASA Issued Disconnection Notices While Communities Had No Water to Use
On May 26, 2026 — while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation and multiple communities had been experiencing zero water access for weeks — NAWASA issued a disconnection notice advising that accounts over 30 days in arrears were liable for disconnection, with reconnection not guaranteed within 72 hours.

No cross-reference to the ongoing supply disruption appeared in the disconnection notice. No exemption was offered. No acknowledgment that billing periods overlapped with documented service restoration variances.

This is not a billing department error in isolation. It is evidence of institutional silos operating without coordination — the same structural pattern that produced fragmented advisories, open-ended timelines, and omitted communities throughout this 134-day record.
