This report documents the first 37 community-generated water signal reports collected between May 15–June 6, 2026 across Grenada parishes. Data was submitted voluntarily by residents through the Water Signal Tracker™ community reporting platform. This pilot demonstrates the existence of a critical real-time water intelligence gap between utility-declared schedules and actual household-level water access — a gap this system is uniquely designed to close.

Key Signal Findings
01
St. George's is the Crisis Concentration Zone
15 of 18 reports (83%) came from St. George's parish. 6 communities reported zero water access — not reduced supply, zero access. This confirms St. George's as the highest-pressure failure zone in the current outage cycle.
02
Elevation Drives Severity — Hilltop Communities Hit Hardest
100% of "No Water" reports came from hilltop or hillside communities. Mt. Parnassus, St. Paul's, La Borie, and Morne Delice — all elevated — reported the longest durations without water. This confirms the pressure-zone imbalance pattern predicted by infrastructure analysis.
03
Duration Severity Is Alarming — "Over a Month" Reports Documented
13 reports documented water outages exceeding one month across Morne Delice and La Borie. This exceeds routine dry season management and signals a systemic supply failure, not a temporary valve regulation event.
04
Notice Accuracy Gap Confirmed
NAWASA's published valve regulation schedule declared supply availability from 5:00 a.m.–3:00 p.m. daily. Community reports submitted during that window show zero water access — confirming a measurable gap between declared supply and actual household delivery.
05
Flatland Communities Show Mixed Results
Calliste (flatland) reported weak pressure while River Road (flatland) reported normal flow — confirming that proximity to supply source and pipe routing, not elevation alone, affects distribution. This data point supports pressure-zone engineering as a targeted intervention.
06
NAWASA Proactive Outreach Confirmed — Reactive Delivery Model Documented 💧 Outreach Recognized
On May 29, 2026, a Morne Delice resident reported that NAWASA contacted them directly by phone to offer a water truck delivery — tank or barrel top-up by delivery truck. This confirms NAWASA has awareness of supply failures at the community level and is managing them through reactive delivery rather than supply restoration. The gap is not knowledge — it is operational response design.
07
June 2 — Manual Valve Control Confirmed at Morne Delice
On June 2, 2026, Morne Delice documented three separate supply events in a single day — 10:40 AM (pulsating/intermittent), 2:30–4:30 PM (trickle, 2 hours), and 8:00–10:00 PM (strong flow, 2 hours then off). Strong pressure at 8:00 PM followed by complete cutoff at 10:00 PM confirms manual valve operation — not a pressure or infrastructure failure. Water supply is physically available but being restricted at the valve level.
08
Advisory Notice Restoration Failure — 13+ Hours Past Promised Deadline ⚠ New Finding
On June 4, 2026, NAWASA issued a Water Supply Interruption Advisory for the Morne Delice Water System, citing low water levels at the Morne Delice Tank. Affected areas listed: Morne Delice, Calivigny, Egmont, Marian, Confer, Hope Vale, and Lower La Borie. Restoration was promised by 11:00 AM AST, Friday June 5, 2026. As of June 6, 2026 at 12:17 AM AST — 13+ hours past the declared deadline — water had not been restored. No follow-up advisory was issued. Grenville was not included in the advisory despite active community reports of disruption, confirming that the gap between affected areas and officially acknowledged areas remains unresolved.

09
St. Andrew Parish — First Normal Flow Signal Documented
A resident in Paradise, St. Andrew (flatland elevation) submitted a report confirming normal water service with no complaints — the first St. Andrew signal in this pilot. This establishes a geographic baseline: flatland communities in St. Andrew are currently receiving uninterrupted service while elevated St. George's communities remain in active crisis. This contrast strengthens the pressure-zone and parish-distribution findings.
