⚠ Critical Finding

This report was built entirely from NAWASA's own public Facebook posts — official advisories, valve regulation notices, and system alerts — cross-referenced with timestamped community observations. No data in this report was fabricated or estimated. Every finding is traceable to a public institutional source. This Second Evidence Report documents 35+ NAWASA advisories issued between February 13 and June 17, 2026 — 134 consecutive days — affecting communities across Grenada. Unlike the First Evidence Report, which documented community-submitted signal data, this report is built entirely from institutional source material: official NAWASA notices, valve regulation schedules, broken main alerts, and system recharge announcements. The data reveals a systemic pattern of cascading service restoration variances, missed restoration timelines, inadequate community notification, and a disconnection between declared service availability and actual household water access. One community — Morne Delice, St. George's — appears in NAWASA's own notices as early as March 2, 2026, and remains unresolved as of the date of this report.

Institutional Pattern Findings
F1
The Crisis Began in February — Not May
The first documented advisory affecting the Morne Delice area dates to February 13, 2026. By March 2, NAWASA issued a formal valve regulation schedule specifically naming La Borie, Mt. Airy, Morne Delice, Mt. Parnassus, The Bocas, Creighton, Holder Hill, and Parade under the Les Avocats Water System. The crisis that community reports captured in May was not new — it had been building institutionally for at least 95 days before the First Evidence Report was published.
F2
NAWASA Issued Disconnection Notices During Active Supply Disruption
On May 26, 2026 at 8:49 AM, while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation and multiple communities had been without water for weeks, NAWASA issued a notice advising that disconnection officers would be operating in listed communities during the week of June 1st. Accounts in arrears over 30 days were liable for disconnection. No exemption or acknowledgment of the ongoing supply disruption was included in the notice.
F3
Production Conditions Were Disclosed 23 Days After Communities Were Already Affected
NAWASA's comprehensive dry season advisory on May 13, 2026 disclosed production conditions of -40% at Les Avocats, -47% at Petit Etang, and -69% at Bon Accord. Community members in areas served by these systems had already been without consistent water for weeks before this disclosure. The institutional knowledge existed before the public was informed.
F4
Advisory Restoration Timelines Were Routinely Revised Without Follow-Up
Across the 134-day record, multiple advisories included specific restoration estimates that were not met without follow-up communication: May 27 advisory — restoration estimate 2:00 PM May 28 for Corinth/Bailles Bacolet/La Sagesse corridor; May 29 advisory — restoration estimate 4:00 PM May 30 for Cuma/Hope/Mt. Fann; June 4 advisory — restoration estimate 11:00 AM June 5 for Morne Delice system — community-reported as unrestored as of June 6 at 12:17 AM. In most instances, no follow-up advisory was issued when an original estimate required revision.
F5
Communities Were Omitted From Advisories Despite Active Disruption
Multiple instances documented where communities experienced disruption without appearing in official notices: Morne Delice on April 4 — resident confirmed Day 6 without water, community not on NAWASA's published list; Grenville on June 5 — public comment confirmed water gone since before midday, Grenville not included in the June 4 advisory; Hope Vale — community member confirmed disruption outside the official schedule window. The advisories systematically undercount the affected population.
F6
The Brandon Hall Dam Tampering Incident Was a National Water Security Event — With No Definitive Restoration Timeline
On May 14, 2026, the Brandon Hall Dam was deliberately tampered with, resulting in complete water loss from the facility. NAWASA stated: "the Authority is unable to provide a definitive timeframe for restoration." This event affected La Digue, Holy Innocence, Union, Battle Hill, Caruth, The Villa, Harford Village, and Cook Hill, and placed additional cascading pressure on an already stressed network.
F7
St. George's University Deployed Its Own Water Tanker — Confirming Supplemental Response Was Required
In the May 13 comprehensive advisory, NAWASA disclosed that St. George's University had officially deployed its water tanker and operator to assist with water deliveries in the southern network. This represents an observable instance of a private institution supplementing public utility operations during the disruption period.
F8
Advisories Were Issued in Silos — No Single Notice Connected the Full Island Picture
Every advisory in this 134-day record addresses a single system, community cluster, or incident. Not one advisory connects the cumulative impact across all 26 systems. A resident reading any individual notice would have no way of knowing that 12 systems were simultaneously under regulation. The fragmented advisory structure is itself a visibility gap. Water Signal Tracker™ is the only platform that connects this data across systems, parishes, and time.
F9
"Effective Immediately and Until Further Notice" — The Language of Indefinite Disruption
Multiple valve regulation schedules in this record were issued with the phrase "effective immediately and until further notice" — including the March 19 Morne Delice Valve Regulation Schedule and the May 26 Munich Water System notice. This language provides no restoration commitment, no review date, and no accountability mechanism. Communities placed under open-ended regulation have no institutional timeline to plan around.
⚠ Accountability Finding — Disconnection During Active Disruption
NAWASA Issued Disconnection Notices While Multiple Communities Had No Water Access
On May 26, 2026 — while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation — NAWASA issued a disconnection notice advising that accounts over 30 days in arrears were liable for disconnection, with reconnection not guaranteed within 72 hours. No cross-reference to the ongoing supply disruption appeared in the disconnection notice. No exemption was offered. This is evidence of institutional silos operating without coordination — the same structural pattern that produced fragmented advisories, unmet restoration estimates, and omitted communities throughout this 134-day record.

Observation — June 15–17 · Operational Pattern
Within a 48-hour window, June 15–17, 2026, NAWASA issued advisories covering five distinct operational categories: broken water mains, high turbidity, planned network maintenance, bulk meter replacement, and restoration timeline revision. Systems affected span St. David, St. John, and St. Andrew parishes. Two notices carried no restoration timeline. This concentration of concurrent multi-system events across multiple parishes within a single 48-hour period represents an observable operational pattern — distinct event types producing similar community outcomes: interrupted or degraded household water access with limited advance planning ability.

New Finding · F10
F10
Restoration Timeline Revision Events and Community Service Outcomes New
On June 15, 2026, NAWASA issued an advisory for the Petite Bacaye / Red Gate / La Calome / Old Westerhall / Westerhall Heights corridor in St. David, with a restoration estimate of 8:00 PM that evening. On June 16, a follow-up notice revised the restoration timeline to 12:00 noon. Community-reported service conditions indicate water was restored to the corridor approximately 27 hours after the original estimate. Initial restoration was followed by community reports of reduced pressure.

This sequence documents a distinction between announced restoration timelines and observed household service conditions — the core measurement gap Water Signal Tracker™ was designed to make visible.

Notably, this event marks the first instance in the 134-day record where NAWASA issued a follow-up communication when an original restoration estimate required revision. This represents an observable change in communication behavior worth tracking going forward.
