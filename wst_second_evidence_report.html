Institutional Pattern Findings
F1
The Crisis Began in February — Not May
The first documented advisory affecting the Morne Delice area dates to February 13, 2026 — St. Paul's and surrounding communities. By March 2, NAWASA issued a formal valve regulation schedule specifically naming La Borie, Mt. Airy, Morne Delice, Mt. Parnassus, The Bocas, Creighton, Holder Hill, and Parade under the Les Avocats Water System. The crisis that community reports captured in May was not new — it had been building institutionally for at least 95 days before the First Evidence Report was published.
F2
NAWASA Issued Disconnection Threats During Active Supply Crisis
On May 26, 2026 at 8:49 AM, while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation and multiple communities had been without water for weeks, NAWASA issued a notice advising that disconnection officers would be operating in listed communities during the week of June 1st — threatening service disconnection for accounts over 30 days in arrears. Customers were being threatened with disconnection for non-payment of a service they were not receiving. No exemption or acknowledgment of the ongoing supply crisis was included in the disconnection notice.
F3
Production Crisis Was Known Since May 13 — Publicly Disclosed 23 Days After Communities Were Already Affected
NAWASA's comprehensive dry season advisory on May 13, 2026 disclosed production deficits of 40% at Les Avocats, 47% at Petit Etang, and 69% at Bon Accord — facilities that had been failing since at least early May. Community members in areas served by these systems had already been without consistent water for weeks before this disclosure. The institutional knowledge existed before the public was informed.
F4
Advisory Restoration Deadlines Were Routinely Missed Without Follow-Up
Across the 117-day record, multiple advisories promised specific restoration times that were not met: May 27 advisory promised restoration by 2:00 PM May 28 for Corinth/Bailles Bacolet/La Sagesse corridor; May 29 advisory promised restoration by 4:00 PM May 30 for Cuma/Hope/Mt. Fann; June 4 advisory promised restoration by 11:00 AM June 5 for Morne Delice system — confirmed unrestored as of June 6 at 12:17 AM. In no instance was a follow-up advisory issued when a deadline was missed.
F5
Communities Were Omitted From Advisories Despite Active Disruption
Multiple instances documented where communities experienced disruption without appearing in official notices: Morne Delice on April 4 — resident confirmed Day 6 without water, community not on NAWASA's published list that day; Grenville on June 5 — public comment confirmed water gone since before midday, Grenville not included in the June 4 advisory; Hope Vale — community member commented on March 19 Morne Delice valve regulation post: "But Hope Vale hasn't had water at..." — confirming disruption outside the official schedule window. The advisories systematically undercount the affected population.
F6
The Brandon Hall Dam Tampering Incident Was a National Water Security Event — With No Definitive Restoration Timeline
On May 14, 2026, the Brandon Hall Dam was deliberately tampered with, resulting in complete water loss from the facility. NAWASA stated at the time of announcement: "the Authority is unable to provide a definitive timeframe for restoration." This event — a deliberate act of infrastructure sabotage — affected La Digue, Holy Innocence, Union, Battle Hill, Caruth, The Villa, Harford Village, and Cook Hill, and placed additional cascading pressure on an already critically stressed network.
F7
St. George's University Deployed Its Own Water Tanker — Confirming NAWASA's Operational Capacity Was Insufficient
In the May 13 comprehensive advisory, NAWASA disclosed that St. George's University had officially deployed its water tanker and operator to assist with water deliveries in the southern network. This is a significant institutional signal: a private university deploying its own emergency infrastructure to supplement a national utility's operations confirms that NAWASA's internal response capacity was inadequate to meet the scale of the crisis.
F8
Advisories Were Issued in Silos — No Single Notice Connected the Full Island Picture
Every advisory in this 117-day record addresses a single system, a single community cluster, or a single incident. Not one advisory connects the cumulative impact across all 26 systems. A resident reading any individual notice would have no way of knowing that 12 systems were simultaneously under regulation, that production deficits exceeded 60% at multiple facilities, or that the same communities were being affected by overlapping system failures. The fragmented advisory structure is itself an accountability gap. Water Signal Tracker™ is the only platform that connects this data across systems, parishes, and time.
F9
"Effective Immediately and Until Further Notice" — The Language of Indefinite Disruption
Multiple valve regulation schedules in this record were issued with the phrase "effective immediately and until further notice" — including the March 19 Morne Delice Valve Regulation Schedule and the May 26 Munich Water System notice. This language provides no restoration commitment, no review date, and no accountability mechanism. Communities placed under open-ended regulation have no institutional recourse and no timeline to plan around.
⚠ Accountability Finding — Disconnection During Crisis
NAWASA Threatened to Disconnect Customers Who Had No Water to Use
On May 26, 2026 — while 12 of 26 water systems were under valve regulation and multiple communities had been experiencing zero water access for weeks — NAWASA issued a disconnection notice advising that accounts over 30 days in arrears were liable for disconnection, with reconnection not guaranteed within 72 hours.

The logical question this raises: If a customer has had no water for 30+ days, what service are they in arrears for? No cross-reference to the ongoing supply crisis appeared in the disconnection notice. No exemption was offered. No acknowledgment that billing periods overlapped with documented supply failures.

This is not a billing department error in isolation. It is evidence of institutional silos operating without coordination — the same structural problem that produced fragmented advisories, missed deadlines, and omitted communities throughout this 117-day record.
